Agent evaluation · RCV-Bench

Can an AI agent verify a research claim?

A 14-task benchmark separates simple rerunning from harder provenance and robustness judgments. The naive rerun baseline scores 9/14 but misses every FRAGILE and FABRICATED case; a first autonomous local-agent baseline catches one fabricated case but remains weak overall.

The point is diagnostic, not leaderboard theater: can an agent distinguish a reproduced result from a deviation, unsupported evidence, or a result that collapses under a declared robustness test?

Evaluation reliability · Inspect AI

Measure whether a benchmark score is stable

Eval Invariance Engine wraps multiple-choice evaluations with semantics-preserving perturbations and reports score drift and item-level flip rates inside Inspect AI.

It measures a narrow question directly: how much does the evaluation result depend on representation details that are not supposed to change the underlying task?

AI evaluation · MMLU

Option-order robustness

The regenerated Qwen harness changes its underlying answer on roughly 78% of sampled questions under four cyclic option orderings in both bf16 and fp32.

The headline robustness failure regenerated while several historical stability and calibration quantities did not. Tie-breaking and dtype controls did not remove the effect; the unresolved quantities remain explicitly unresolved.

Reasoning evaluation · ARC-AGI-2

When candidate selection helps but generation still fails

Corrected same-holdout analysis puts k=1 demonstration reliability at 32.8%. MDL improves ambiguous-case selection by 11.1 points over random, while the candidate oracle is only 3.7 points above MDL.

The public correction overturned the earlier optimistic calibration story. The frozen solver remains 0/167; the contribution is the measurement and selection analysis, not a competitive ARC solver.

Case 002 · real regeneration

MMLU: when the headline survives but supporting metrics do not

The majority-flip finding regenerated; historical stability and calibration metrics did not.

The lab's rule is simple: a historical result, a regenerated result, and an independently verified result are different evidence states. The job is not to validate a preferred story; it is to reduce decision-relevant uncertainty.

How the work is built

Reproduce before interpretingMatch the published result before diagnosing where it comes from.
Calibrate selectionAccount for the search that identified the most influential observation.
Freeze provenanceBind claims to versioned code, artifacts, checksums, and explicit status.
Publish limitsSeparate completed evidence, reconstructed results, and work still awaiting independent rerun.

These projects are independent analyses. They narrow what the public evidence supports; they do not substitute authority for verification or turn a localized hint into a discovery claim.
