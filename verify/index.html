A headline metric can be true in its original harness and materially weaker after comparator normalization, transfer testing, or fully loaded cost normalization. The question is not only “is the number real?” but “what decision does it actually support?”

What I check

Four tests, asked in order

Each one isolates a different way a represented advantage can lose underwriting value.

01 · REAL

Does it reproduce?

Reconstruct the represented measurement from the strongest available source artifacts without silently changing the protocol.

02 · PROPRIETARY

What belongs to the target?

Normalize the comparator and separate base-model, scaffold, architecture, data, and configuration effects where the evidence permits.

03 · DURABLE

Does it survive transfer?

Re-measure on fresh customers, shifted distributions, later periods, or declared semantics-preserving perturbations.

04 · ECONOMIC

What is fully loaded?

Bridge narrow technical metrics to retries, failed attempts, human review, fallback, and other costs that matter to the economic thesis.

Why trust the method

The discipline runs in public

On RCV-Bench, a deterministic policy that simply re-runs the headline result gets 9/14 verdicts right while finding 0/3 fragile cases and 0/2 fabricated cases. That is the gap between re-execution and verification.
