What I check

Four tests, asked in order

Each one is a place a represented advantage tends to leak. Together they separate a real moat from a mispriced premium.

01 · REAL

Does it reproduce outside the seller’s environment?

Rebuild the claim from the evidence in the room and run it independently. A result that only holds in the vendor’s own harness is a marketing artifact, not an asset.

02 · PROPRIETARY

Advantage, or a handicapped comparator?

A large share of most “we beat the field by X” claims is a weak or misconfigured baseline. Normalize it and watch how much of the gap survives.

03 · DURABLE

Does it survive transfer?

Re-measure on fresh customers, shifted distributions, and later time periods. An edge that needs its home cohort is not a moat you can underwrite for a five-year hold.

04 · ECONOMIC

Narrow, or fully loaded?

Bridge the unit economics from the story to reality — inference cost, retention, comparator and key-person effects — to the margin you’d actually earn.

Why trust the method

The same discipline runs in public

Pointed at research claims, the method is fully checkable. On RCV-Bench, an agent that merely re-runs the code looks competent — and is blind to the two failure modes that destroy premiums:

9/14overall — looks competent 0/3fragile — collapses under a stated perturbation 0/2fabricated — no backing artifact

The public reproductions behind this work — ARC-AGI-2, DESI DR2, MMLU — are inspectable, with frozen results and explicit limits. In an externally graded reproduction (NeurIPS 2026 FAIR Universe, Codabench), I caught a score-orientation bug in the official baseline: the kind of silent error that inverts a conclusion while the headline number stays plausible.
