# PAVE — Portfolio AI Value Engine

## Prove AI value before you scale it

Private-equity portfolio companies are under pressure to use AI, but most AI programs still struggle to answer a basic operating question:

> **Did this actually create measurable value — or did it just move work around?**

PAVE is a controlled value-creation method for identifying one high-value workflow, testing an AI-enabled operating design against a frozen baseline, and deciding whether to **deploy, repair, or kill** the intervention before broader rollout.

PAVE is not a generic AI transformation program. It is designed for portfolio-operations teams that need evidence, controls, and repeatability.

---

## How PAVE works

**1. Baseline the workflow**  
Map the existing process, human touches, cycle time, policy rules, exceptions, and operating cost.

**2. Separate interpretation from control**  
AI handles messy evidence interpretation, summarization, routing, and recommendation. Deterministic systems retain control of pricing, arithmetic, permissions, contractual thresholds, and other irreversible or policy-sensitive decisions.

**3. Run in shadow mode**  
The system is tested on historical or parallel cases before it is allowed to affect customers, contracts, or financial records.

**4. Score the result**  
Measure accuracy, escalation quality, false autonomy, human-review burden, cycle time, and normalized economic value.

**5. Deploy, repair, or kill**  
Only workflows that clear the control and value gates move toward production.

---

## Case 001 — Renewal Operations

PAVE’s first completed pattern tested renewal operations for a synthetic PE-backed recurring-revenue software company.

The first version exposed a critical design flaw: the AI model correctly classified risk and routing, but produced unreliable commercial pricing when asked to own the arithmetic.

PAVE was redesigned around a hybrid architecture:

> **AI interprets operating evidence. Deterministic controls own money, permissions, and hard policy rules.**

The repaired benchmark used 64 randomized renewal cases across two policy configurations, including overlapping exceptions, account mismatches, custom terms, amendment conflicts, discount thresholds, delinquency thresholds, stale data, missing usage, expansion signals, and retention-risk conditions.

The hybrid system cleared the precommitted evaluation gate, including zero false-autonomy failures and full deterministic pricing accuracy in the synthetic test environment.

This does **not** establish production ROI. It establishes that the architecture is technically viable enough to justify a controlled portfolio-company pilot.

## What a portfolio-company pilot looks like

PAVE starts small:

**One portfolio company. One workflow. One controlled test.**

Typical pilot sequence:

- select a workflow with measurable volume and economic relevance;
- establish the human baseline;
- ingest a limited historical dataset;
- define deterministic controls and the AI autonomy envelope;
- run shadow-mode cases;
- quantify errors, escalations, review burden, and capacity release;
- translate results into a normalized economic bridge;
- issue a clear **deploy / repair / kill** recommendation.

No production autonomy is required for the pilot. No customer-facing action is needed to determine whether the opportunity is real.

---

## What PAVE measures

PAVE does not treat theoretical hours saved as EBITDA.

The economic bridge separates:

> **avoided labor + incremental gross profit + avoided vendor spend − AI/model costs − human review/escalation cost − implementation cost − expected error/rework cost**

Results distinguish among:

- released employee capacity;
- actual cost reduction;
- retained or expanded gross profit;
- working-capital effects;
- one-time savings;
- implementation burden;
- ongoing model/infrastructure cost;
- human-review and exception cost.

If the evidence does not support an economic claim, PAVE does not make it.

## Why the portfolio matters

The long-term value is not one successful agent.

Every validated workflow becomes a **PAVE Pattern Card** documenting:

**company archetype → workflow → required systems/data → AI architecture → deterministic controls → autonomy envelope → implementation burden → measured result → failure modes → rollout prerequisites → portability**

The objective is a reusable portfolio AI value-creation library so that each new portfolio company does not start from zero.

Renewal Operations is Pattern Card #001.

---

## Best first fit

PAVE is best suited initially to PE-backed businesses with:

- recurring or transaction-heavy operating workflows;
- meaningful manual preparation or review burden;
- structured source systems such as CRM, billing, ERP, support, or contract repositories;
- policy-sensitive decisions that can be bounded by deterministic controls;
- sufficient historical volume to establish a real baseline;
- an operating team willing to run a controlled shadow test before production deployment.

Vertical SaaS and other recurring-revenue software businesses are especially strong initial candidates.

## The ask

> **Give PAVE one portfolio company and one operating workflow. We will prove or falsify the AI value-creation opportunity before you scale it.**

The output is not a strategy deck. It is an evidence-backed operating decision: **deploy, repair, or kill — with the controls, economics, and implementation requirements documented for reuse across the portfolio.**
